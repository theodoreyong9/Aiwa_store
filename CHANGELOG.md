# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Video catalogue: first real pass (39 campaigns), then fixes.** The pass read 39 campaigns (ideas, credits and links all came through), but the sector was found for only 13: the page's sentence is worded in more than one way. The record now keeps that sentence (`facts`), finds the sector and the media in the other wordings, section links such as `/campaigns/new` are no longer taken for campaigns, a record written before is read again (a few per run) to be completed, and the sync prints its result as clean JSON (the log goes to stderr).
- **A catalogue of advertising campaigns for the script of a video (`video-research/`, backend 51).** A weekly workflow reads, politely, the campaigns that Ads of the World shows (its `robots.txt` allows the home, campaigns and campaign pages; every request is checked against it, an unreadable file means nothing is fetched): the idea in a short excerpt, the credits, the sector, the page and the address of the film. No film or picture of a film is downloaded or stored. `research "<brief>"` brings back the closest campaigns (a few French words are carried over to English). The instruction of the video (stage 1) points to the playbook and to this search, and asks the session to cite 2 or 3 campaigns in `brief.md`. Vimeo (its terms forbid automated access outside its API) and The One Show (`Disallow: /` for generic bots) were looked at and left out; LBB answers 403 from the servers of GitHub; YouTube and Vimeo refuse them for downloads.
- **A director's playbook (`docs/DIRECTOR-PLAYBOOK.md`).** The rules of the craft for a short film: the hook, the five beats, rhythm, text on screen, sound, colour and type, honesty, and the critique sheet. General craft knowledge, not measured on a corpus of films (it says so).
- **A film analyzer (`video-kit/src/analyze.py`).** It reads a film and writes a report of how it is made: the shots (where it cuts, their lengths, colour, movement), the rhythm (average and median shot, the first cut, the pace by tenth, whether it speeds up), the movement per second, the sound (loudness, silences, tempo estimate) and the colours, with notes in words computed from the numbers. Pure Python and ffmpeg. The film is only read; the report keeps measurements and our own notes, never a picture or a sound of the film. Tested on a synthetic film with known cuts (found exactly). Known limit: it finds hard cuts, and understates soft or low-contrast transitions (`--scene` adjusts).
- **A short Aiwa video (1 min 24 s), made with the cinematic engine and following the published video, is added next to the long one (which stays).** `docs/aiwa-promo-cine-16x9.mp4` and `docs/aiwa-promo-cine-9x16.mp4` (about 6 MB each), with their sources in `docs/video-aiwa/` (the shot list and the voice-over), shown in the Papers tab as "The short version". The engine gets an `html` layer (any HTML at a design size, animated by a `tick` function), `x`, `y` and `w` that can differ by format, and a fix for lines that left a mask without disappearing; with tests.
- **Promotional videos: a cinematic engine and a production in stages (backend 50).** The kit only had a slideshow engine (a title and a picture per scene), and the instruction said "make a video" in one go. Now: `cinematic.js` + `motion.js` (kinetic text, camera moves, real pages scrolled, device frames, counters, drawing paths, eight transitions, grain), `capture` (a page as one tall picture), `beat.py` (a pulse with an accent on every cut), `voice.py` (a local voice-over, the music ducks under it), and a check that warns of a `slideshow` (the old Yo video: 51% of its half-seconds barely move; the cinematic one: none). The instruction and `docs/PROMO-VIDEO.md` make it a production in stages (facts, brief, script, shot list, sound, preview, critique with scores, final), each written to a file, with a critique pass on a contact sheet. The voice is espeak-ng (robotic) unless piper is set up; the music is generated.
- **The design catalogue can be grown in batches.** The `Design catalogue` workflow takes the number of listing pages read per registry and the projects kept (default unchanged: 2 pages, 30), so that older projects of Awwwards and CSS Winner can be added without hammering them (1.5 s between requests, a bounded number of visits per run).
- **References are traced (backend 49).** The design instruction asks the session to write `aiwa-design/references.md` with the 2 or 3 references it kept: address, catalogue `id`, the screenshots looked at (raw addresses), the measurements taken from the catalogue, what the pictures show that the measurements do not, and what was taken and where it shows. - **The ceiling of the reward formula is gone.** `rewardFixed` returned nothing, so a gain of 0, for any single result above 10^12, instead of limiting it: it did not bound AIWA's production (that is not defined: each reader counts what it accepts), and it would have paid the biggest producer nothing. It was never reachable with the reference parameters (billions of years for 1 SOL, thousands of years for a million). Nothing else changes: there is still no maximum supply.
- **The wallet no longer waits between epochs.** It used to work one epoch every 30 s, a timer of the reference wallet that nothing in the protocol asks for: a client without the wait earned more with the same capital, and nobody had a reason to keep it. The wallet now works back to back, as fast as the device can, and signs one event for what it worked in about 30 s (`progress.eventMs` in `deployment.json`; the number of epochs per event adapts to the device, so the log grows as slowly as before). It does not work before its first commitment: those epochs earned nothing and only raised the age, which slows what is accrued afterwards. Production in AIWA now depends on the speed of the device's core (measured here: 2.4 s per epoch in JavaScript, about 12 times the old pace); `progress.intervalMs` still gives one epoch per interval, for tests and demos. The figures of the yellow paper and the explainers stay counted at 120 epochs per hour and say so. The battery and the heat of a phone are not measured.
- **An app that uses the wallet can no longer pay without the player.** The door (`apps/web/src/app-door.js`) paid with no question asked, no cap and no expiry: an app that declared `<meta name="aiwa-wallet">` could empty the wallet as soon as it was open, with a banner as the only signal. Now the first `pay` of an app opens a sheet of the Store (not of the app) saying who asks, how much, to whom: the player refuses, allows that payment, or allows a **budget** until the app is closed. Inside the budget the payments go through (the click duel still plays smoothly), the first one beyond it asks again, a refusal makes the app's next requests fail for 30 s without asking, and closing the app (or Escape) answers the question with a refusal. Payments still are signed, each one, by the channel's session key; what changed is that the player's consent comes first. Two e2e tests: the door (the sheet, the budget, a budget too small for the payment, a refusal, closing while asked) and the click duel (the loser is asked, the winner is not). Yellow paper §17.2, §18.8, §20, the plain-words explanation and the plan say so. Not tried on a phone.

- The wallet starts by itself and the Recovery panel is gone: the first tap creates it, the 12 words are kept in the phone's keystore, its history comes back by itself (Android's backup, archive nodes if the deployment lists any, the registry's public baseline of the author). The registry now publishes `store/baselines/<author>.json`.
- Two kinds of app in the store, both submitted on GitHub: `code` (the package carries the HTML) and `aiwa` (the package carries a pointer to a signed bundle published through Aiwa; the registry and the Store both verify it against the pinned manifest id). The Aiwa SDK is built as one module (`lib/aiwa.js`) for such apps, with an example.
- Publishing from the widget: the Publish tab is replaced by one sheet, opened by the ▦ button, that signs with the wallet and opens the pull request on the author's GitHub account (GitHub's device flow, run by the Android app; the token and the 12 words in the Keystore). A browser hands the signed file over instead.
- `android`: the `aiwa` deploy mode next to `store` (backend 73 tests), the `github-login` and secret commands of the host channel, automatic backup (secrets excluded).
- Apps that use the wallet: an app that declares `<meta name="aiwa-wallet">` gets a banner and a door to the page (who the player is, pay through a channel session key, receive, show or scan a code). The **click duel** (`docs/demo-apps/click-duel.html`): two phones linked by WebRTC through two codes, a price per click, 20 seconds, the one who clicked less pays what they clicked, nothing signed per click. Web tests 37 (20 in Chromium).
- Fix, `aiwa-lib`: the wallet's folded state could be left stale when a read overlapped a mining step or a confirmed burn (the commitment then looked unbacked, the position vanished from the screen and from payments). Reads of the state now go one at a time and a fold overtaken by a reset is done again. A test that interleaves them (it failed three times in four before the fix). lib 100 tests.
- Documentation rewritten around how the system works (README with real screenshots and the install steps, plain-words explanation in English and French, the yellow paper in seven parts); every diagram and every `§` reference is checked in CI (`scripts/check-diagrams.mjs`).
- Workflows: *Pages* and *Devnet check* fail, with the reason and the link, instead of passing without doing anything.
- The Store reads QR codes with the camera: decoded in JavaScript (jsQR, tested in Chromium against a fake camera), and `StoreActivity` grants the camera to the page's own origin, for video only, after Android's permission (compiled in CI, never run on a phone). Web tests 40.
- The widget's logo opens the app (the Store), not the dictation text screen; inside the app a **Dictate** tab opens that screen.
- The README's introduction: a distributed ecosystem (build, submit, store, token, play and pay), not only a store.
- Layout from the bottom of the page: the title and the tabs are a bar at the bottom of the screen, a page's content grows upwards from it, the Store's search field is the lowest thing; the viewer's bar (name, Close) is at the bottom too. The README's screenshots are redrawn.
- The click duel says plainly where Solana comes in: the game never touches it; the wallet checks once that received AIWA comes from a real burn, and with no connection a payment counts once the phone has been online (`receive` now answers `pending`).
